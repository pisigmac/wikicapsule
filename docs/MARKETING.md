# Marketing — Product Positioning

## Elevator Pitch

WikiCapsule turns your markdown notes into a compounding knowledge base that any AI assistant can read, search, and update. It's git-backed, runs entirely on your machine, and works with every major AI platform through the Model Context Protocol.

## Target Users

| Segment | Pain Point | How WikiCapsule Helps |
|---------|-----------|----------------------|
| AI Researchers | Knowledge scattered across chat sessions | Persistent, growing wiki |
| Technical Writers | Hard to maintain evolving docs | Git-backed with auto-indexing |
| Engineering Teams | Tribal knowledge lost when people leave | Shared, versioned knowledge base |
| Consultants | Reusing insights across clients | Template wikis per engagement |
| Students | Notes don't compound across semesters | Long-term knowledge building |

## Key Differentiators

1. **MCP-native**: Works with Claude, GPT-4, Gemini, and any future MCP client
2. **Git-backed**: Full version history, branching, collaboration
3. **Local-first**: No cloud dependency, no API keys for search
4. **Provider-agnostic**: Not tied to any single AI vendor
5. **Open source**: MIT license, community extensible

## Positioning Statement

For knowledge workers who use AI assistants, WikiCapsule is an MCP server that turns markdown files into a compounding knowledge base. Unlike Notion or Obsidian, it's built for the AI-native workflow where your assistant can read, write, and maintain your knowledge automatically.

## Messaging Pillars

### Pillar 1: Compounding Knowledge

"Every conversation makes your wiki smarter."

Your AI assistant reads from your wiki, adds to it, and keeps it organized. Over time, your knowledge base becomes more valuable — not more chaotic.

### Pillar 2: You Own Your Data

"Markdown + Git. No lock-in, no cloud required."

Your knowledge lives in plain text files under version control. You can read them with any text editor, sync them with GitHub, and migrate away at any time.

### Pillar 3: Works With Every AI

"One wiki, every platform."

Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, local models — if it speaks MCP, it can use your wiki. No vendor-specific integrations needed.
